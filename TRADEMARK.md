# Trademark and branding

**In one paragraph:**

> Sidelong™ and the Sidelong logo are trademarks of the project owner. The source
> code may be used, modified, and redistributed according to the project's
> [software licence](LICENSE). However, redistribution or modified versions must
> not use the Sidelong name, logo, or other branding in a way that suggests they
> are the official Sidelong project.

The rest of this file is that same rule, spelled out.

The **code** in this repository is covered by [`LICENSE`](LICENSE). This file
covers something the licence deliberately does not: the **name and the mark**.

Copyright licences and trademarks protect different things, and mixing them up
is the usual mistake. A copyright licence governs *copying the software*. A
trademark governs *what you may call the thing you ship*. Even a licence as
permissive as MIT — which this project used up to and including v0.1.9 — grants
no right whatsoever to the name. That separation is standard practice: the Linux
kernel, Rust, Firefox and PostgreSQL all give the code away and keep the name.

## What you may do

- Use the software under the terms in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE).
- Say your project "works with Sidelong", "is built for Sidelong", or "is
  compatible with Sidelong" — that is nominative use, and it is fine.
- Link to this repository or to the site.
- Quote the name in reviews, articles, comparisons and documentation.

## What you may not do

- **Publish a copy, fork or derivative under the name "Sidelong"**, or any name
  confusingly close to it, in a way that suggests it is this project or comes
  from its maintainer.
- Use the logo or the site's visual identity for your own distribution.
- Imply endorsement, affiliation or authorship you do not have.

If you fork this project, rename it. Keep the attribution the licence requires;
drop the name and the mark.

## Why this exists

The name is the part that actually identifies the project to the people using
it. Someone publishing a stale copy under a different name is a curiosity;
someone publishing one under *this* name is a support burden for its users and a
reputation risk for its maintainer, because when it breaks, the person on the
receiving end will be looking for whoever wrote "Sidelong".

## Status, stated honestly

No trademark is registered at the time of writing. This is a statement of the
maintainer's position and intent, not a claim of a registered mark, and nothing
here is legal advice. Unregistered marks do accrue some common-law protection in
many jurisdictions through use, but that protection is weaker and narrower than
registration.

Questions about use of the name: open an issue on the repository.
